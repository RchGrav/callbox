usage: backmux purge [<target>|all] [-a|--all] [-s|--system] [-u|--user]
                     [--force]

Remove ended processes and their stored records, logs, and screens.
Without a target, sweep eligible processes; with a target, remove that process.

  -a, --all        include stale processes (widens state, not store scope)
  -u, --user       sweep personal processes (the default)
  -s, --system     sweep root-managed processes; invokes sudo if needed
  --force          with a target: remove it even if saved or running
                   without a target: include stale processes, as -a does

With a target, --force stops a running process before removing it. A sweep
never removes saved or running processes, even with --force.

purge all ends every running process that is not saved and removes them all,
with the ended ones, and names the processes it ended. Saved processes are kept:
backmux save is how a process is kept.

Ordinary purge leaves saved processes alone. --name at launch does not save
a process; save and rename do. Removing a record frees its name.
Aliases: rm, prune, drop. To stop a process and retain its record, use end.

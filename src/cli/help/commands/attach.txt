usage: backmux attach <target> [--detach-keys KEYS]

Pick a running process back up in this terminal: a PTY process (backmux <cmd>,
-it, -dt) or one placed with -i. Detach again with Ctrl-P Ctrl-Q. (console is an
alias of attach.)

Without a terminal (piped), attach sends its input and then keeps showing the
process's output until the process ends; it does not end the process's input, so
a -di process can be fed again by the next attach. To drive a process from a
script, backmux send, peek, and wait are usually what you want.

Ctrl-P is a lead key: it waits for the next key, with no time limit. Ctrl-Q
after it detaches; Ctrl-P again sends one Ctrl-P to the program; any other key
sends both through, so nothing typed is lost. --detach-keys KEYS changes the
keys for this attach (ctrl-a,ctrl-d; one key, such as ctrl-], detaches at
once).

One terminal has a process at a time: a second attach is told who has it.
Attached from inside another process (a backmux shell, say), the detach keys let
go of the innermost attach first - the one on screen - and leave you in the
process around it; press them again to detach that one. A console is free again
as soon as its client is gone, however it went (a closed window, a killed
process).
